## Task

OCR this handwritten notebook page. Produce structured output for each separate source on the page.
I will also provide a .json index of all sources processed so far and a .bib bibliography — use these to identify continuations, avoid duplicates, and check for existing bibliography entries.

## How the notebook pages are structured

- Notes on books will begin with a large title, then the author's name and the year of publication. Notes on articles will begin with the article title, a dash, then (usually) the year of publication, generally in blue. The individual notes themselves will generally be in black.
- Page numbers are written in the left margin. Render them as a dash and the number on its own line (e.g. "- 42"), then continue the text on that line. Start a new line for each new page number. Page numbers will always be in order, so if you are producing out-of-order page numbers, double-check and correct your reading.
- Different chapters or sections of the same book are NOT separate sources. Treat them as part of the same source using the book's title, author, and year. Only start a new source entry when the author or book changes entirely.
- If the writing on the page begins with no title, it is likely a continuation of the previous page. At the top of such pages there is usually the author's surname and the year of publication, and each notebook page will have a page number. Use this information together with the .json index to determine which source is being continued. Note that the "page" field in the index may be a range (e.g. "10-14"), meaning the source spans those pages.

## Things to take particular care with

- The notes may contain up and down arrows — render these as the correct Unicode characters (↑ ↓), not as words or substitutes.
- Be *extremely* careful to render numbers and percentages correctly. Take extra time if you need.
- Expand all abbreviations (e.g. "mkt" to "market", "w/o" to "without", "b/c" to "because"). Be very attentive to these as well.
